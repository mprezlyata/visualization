I chose the EarlyON Child and Family Centers dataset: https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/earlyon-child-and-family-centres/
This is a link to my visualization on the Tableau Public: https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/marta.zaiats/viz/EarlyONChildandFamilyCentresLocations/Dashboard1 
    > What software did you use to create your data visualization?
Tableau Public
    > Who is your intended audience? 
General public, especially parents or caregivers who might want a simpler, immediate visual overview.
    > What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization? 
- A map-based visualization that shows the geographical spread of EarlyON Centres across Toronto.
- This helps families/caregivers find EarlyON Child and Family Centers in their neighborhood.
    > What design principles (substantive, perceptual, aesthetic) did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply these principles? With what elements of your plots? 
Substantive:
- The base map is Toronto. The location of each center is represented by a dot.
Perceptual:
- Using a map where each center marked with a dot.
- A legend clarifies what the shades or symbols represent, how to use filters.
Aesthetic:
- Minimal color range used to highlight differences clearly.
- Clear, readable legend; no unnecessary text.
    > How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization? 
- Tableau-based visualizations are partially reproducible if you share the same file with data and instructions for generating the chart. However, it’s not fully automated like a Python script.
- Each user might replicate the steps differently unless very clear instructions are provided.
    > How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?  
- Providing descriptive text for the map.
- Ensuring color choices do not rely solely on red/green contrasts.
    > Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?  
- Families and children: a visual map can help them quickly find the nearest centre.
- Local nonprofits, city officials: can identify coverage gaps in real time.
    > How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization? 
Included: Location-based attributes (coordinates, address), program name, hours of operation, languages, phone number, email address.
Excluded: Operator/agency details in the final map for clarity.
    > What ‘underwater labour’ contributed to your final data visualization product?
Data restructuring to fit into Tableau map chart format, creating parameters, calculated fields.
